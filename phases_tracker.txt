 6. Transactional Outbox Infrastructure (`outbox` package, reviewed against `.claude/agents/outbox-reviewer.md`'s correctness criteria):
    - V23 migration: outbox_events (company_id, aggregate_type/id, event_type, payload TEXT, status, attempts, available_at, published_at, locked_by, last_error).
    - OutboxEventPublisher.publish(...) — Propagation.MANDATORY so the event row can only be written inside a caller's own @Transactional business method (enforces "event write and business write share one transaction" at the type level, not by convention). No business capability calls it yet — pure plumbing.
    - Claim-safe dispatch: OutboxEventRepository.claimBatch is a single atomic native UPDATE ... ORDER BY available_at LIMIT :batchSize tagging claimed rows with a per-run locked_by token (portable MySQL row-locking trick, avoids a MySQL/H2 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED compatibility gamble); OutboxDispatchService.claimBatch/dispatchOne each get their own short @Transactional (separate bean from OutboxDispatcher's @Scheduled entry point, since Spring's proxy-based @Transactional doesn't apply to self-invocation) so row locks aren't held during downstream I/O and one event's outcome doesn't roll back its neighbors.
    - Retry/dead-letter: failure increments attempts, sets last_error (truncated to 2000 chars), and either reschedules available_at with exponential backoff (capped at 5 min) or moves to DEAD_LETTER past app.outbox.max-attempts (default 5). Rows are always updated in place, never deleted.
    - OutboxEventHandler is the pluggable delivery seam for the future Luup consumer; LoggingOutboxEventHandler is the current default (logs + always "succeeds", since there's no real downstream yet).
    - app.outbox.{dispatch-enabled,dispatch-interval-ms,batch-size,max-attempts} in application.yml; dispatch-enabled=false in application-test.yml so the @Scheduled worker doesn't run during tests.
    - Verified for real, not just against H2: booted the app against a throwaway MySQL 8.4 container, confirmed all 23 Flyway migrations (including V23) apply cleanly and the native claim UPDATE runs error-free every scheduling cycle.
